# Results retained for a joint decision

See [context-aware review and resilient content analysis](context-review.md) for policy 3, encrypted-body coverage and semantic deadline controls introduced in 0.20.1.

<a id="score-visible-et-décision"></a>
## Visible score and decision

Since 0.10.2, the journal, its mobile view and detail also display the score of the messages "To be checked" and "Incomplete analysis". The field `score` keeps the index calculated by the engine; `decision.score` can be null when the engine abstains. This does not mean that the engine has not calculated anything.

The console favours decision score when it exists, then the recorded content index. It indicates "Indicative score" for forbearance and "Partial score" for incomplete analysis, with known missing controls and a mention if local extraction is limited. A partial index is not a validated estimate of the entire message. The absent or invalid values remain unavailable; they are never replaced by zero.

This choice of display does not change the ranking, confirmation or actions applied. A malware remains a priority even with a low content index. A delivery notification generated by the server has an internal value, distinct from an incoming traffic analysis. The messages already delivered benefit from this presentation without reanalysis or modification of their history.

<a id="observations-des-moteurs"></a>
## Engine observations

The [SMTP diagnostic headers](message-headers.md) expose the available score, its qualification and missing checks on newly prepared messages. Historical UI presentation does not rewrite old headers.

Since 0.3.0-dev.8, the new analyses keep a `evidence` schema field `noisefence-evidence-1`. It prepares to learn a combination of engines. It does not activate this combination and does not turn the current score into a probability. The old messages keep `evidence: null`; their missing controls are not deduced from the current settings.

<a id="origine-et-états"></a>
## Origin and status

| Origin | Meaning | Export for fusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `smtp_session` | IP and SMTP server envelope | Retained with authorized human feedback |
| `supplied_envelope` | Parameters provided to the program, including with `analyze` | Excluded |
| `content_only` | Local analysis without session, especially with `scan` | Excluded |

States are `disabled`, `not_run`, `complete`, `unavailable`, `busy`, `skipped` and `limited`. `complete` means a result is available, not necessarily successful authentication or legitimate mail. `not_run` distinguishes a configured check that did not run from a disabled check. An absent DKIM observation before verification differs from an empty result list after verifying an unsigned message.

SPF, DKIM and DMARC have individual states. DMARC's SPF and DKIM alignments remain separate. ARC is observed independently, even when other authentication controls are disabled. A valid ARC does not prove Proton's trust in its signatory.

The operations are marked before their asynchronous wait. At the end of the overall delay, the results already received remain preserved: DKIM can be complete while SPF is still waiting for the DNS. Temporary errors of ARC or authentication produce an incomplete analysis and transmission without prefix. The headers provided in the email cannot complete these observations.

<a id="réputation-et-contexte"></a>
## Reputation and context

ZEN requests retain all their return codes. DBL requests also retain their roles: envelope, HELO, visible From, body. The same search can have multiple roles without being counted several times. IPv4/IPv6 addresses mapped use the corresponding ZEN IPv4 search.

Since dev.21, PBL 10/11 and BCL 30 do not add the historical weight +4 of malicious reputation. Their codes remain available for evaluation; the confirmation uses the same interpretation as the score.

The malicious domains and compromised legitimate domains are distinct. DBL 102–106 codes no longer trigger the historical malicious domain weight for a sender identity. In the body links, they remain observed with zero weight waiting for calibration. This follows the context distinction recommended by Spamhaus; no real quality gain is inferred from this single modification.

The connector processes all the codes, with a maximum of 32 entries per response. Error codes, inconsistent zone responses and unsupported codes become unavailability. A negative result is retained only after NXDOMAIN. The positive cache does not prolong the TTL, with a ceiling of 60 seconds; the solver manages its negative cache. The limits remain an IP and twelve domains within the existing global delay. After a first malicious domain, the following searches remain explicitly `not_run`.

DQS remains disabled without a key. Keys, DNS names and raw identities are not copied to `evidence`.

<a id="artefacts-et-confidentialité"></a>
## Artifacts and confidentiality

The SHA-256 of the lexical and semantic models correspond to the loaded bytes. The combination is linked to the already loaded lexical model; a later rereading of the file cannot change its identity. The encoder protocol, the application version, the dependency lock, the detection settings and the LLM prompt are recorded. The settings are hashed without exporting paths, keys, cloud accounts or recipients.

The LLM context contains its category and its declared numbers, without its textual explanation. These are neither human labels nor validated probabilities. The score determining its consultation allows us to study this selection bias. The final historical score is a comparison reference; it must remain excluded from the entries of a fusion learned.

The result of the complementary scanner is retained before it is converted into an advisory signal. Its raw category does not change the delivery policy which limits this scanner to a notice.

ClamD does not attest to the exact set of signatures loaded for each INSTREAM: `antivirus_database_sha256` and `signatures_database_sha256` remain unknown. Nor does the cloud model name guarantee an immutable revision: `llm_model_revision` remains unknown. These limits remain to be processed for full validation; a file on disk or a mutable name does not constitute proof of the artifact actually used.

Observations follow the 30-day retention and existing rights per recipient, including hidden copies. They do not add bodies, recipients, or domain names to the shared view. They are available in the History API and CLI analyses.

## Training

`export-learning` adds `evidence` to the existing fix scheme. It only retains supported and consistent `smtp_session` contexts, and leaves the null field for historical, local or manually provided data. `evidence_exported`, `missing_evidence` and `non_smtp_evidence` meters measure coverage. Incomplete label conflicts, rights, retention and vector exclusions remain checked. An external failure does not remove complete local features of the corpus.

The content trainer ignores this additional field. A fusion trainer will have to demand the expected artifacts, use off-train outputs, process correlations and availability, and then have separate lots for probabilities, threshold and test. Corrections only remain a biased sample.
